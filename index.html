Intro

Hi! Welcome to my page. This website documents my journey in engineering and serves as a personal record of my academic and technical growth. I created this space to continuously organize what I learn, build, and explore while maintaining a clear history of how my engineering interests and skills develop over time.

It is designed as an open and accessible platform where my academic interests, projects, and engineering progress can be easily viewed at a glance.

About Me

I am currently studying Electrical Engineering at Georgia Tech. My interest in engineering developed from a deeper curiosity about how electronic systems operate beyond their visible functions. Rather than viewing devices only as finished products, I became interested in the physical principles behind them—especially how voltage, current, materials, and device structures interact to produce real electronic behavior.

Over time, this curiosity evolved into a broader interest in semiconductor devices, circuits, and the systems built from them. I am particularly interested in how analog and digital components interact within modern semiconductor products such as SoCs and power-management systems. Understanding not only an individual transistor or circuit block, but also how components such as power, timing, control, and digital logic work together within a complete chip has become an increasingly important part of my engineering interests.

My current goal is to develop the perspective of an engineer who can move across these different levels of abstraction—from semiconductor devices and circuits to digital hardware and system architecture. Through my Electrical Engineering coursework, FPGA and digital hardware projects, and continued exploration beyond the classroom, I hope to build a stronger understanding of how modern semiconductor systems are designed as complete products.

This portfolio represents that ongoing process. It documents my effort to explore both the EE and computer-engineering sides of modern hardware while steadily developing the technical foundation and system-level perspective needed to work on advanced semiconductor technologies.

Career Goals

My long-term goal is to become a hardware engineer working on advanced semiconductor systems, with a strong foundation in electrical engineering and an understanding of both device-level behavior and digital system architecture. I am especially interested in SoC-oriented engineering, where semiconductor devices, analog and mixed-signal circuits, digital logic, power management, and computer architecture come together within a single hardware platform.

In the short term, I plan to strengthen my Electrical Engineering foundation through coursework in Circuit Technology and Electronic Devices. I want to develop a deeper understanding of semiconductor devices and analog circuit behavior while studying how important building blocks such as LDO regulators, PLLs, clocking circuits, and other mixed-signal interfaces interact with the larger chip. I am particularly interested in how power sequencing, clock generation, timing, control, and signal integrity influence the operation of an SoC as a complete system.

At the same time, I plan to continue developing my digital hardware skills through FPGA and RTL projects outside the classroom. These projects allow me to explore practical concepts such as hardware interfaces, memory systems, control logic, communication protocols, and hardware/software interaction. By combining this experience with further study of computer architecture, I hope to better understand how digital systems are organized beyond individual RTL modules and how architectural decisions ultimately shape the hardware implementation.

During my undergraduate studies, I also plan to gain exposure to VLSI and CMOS integrated-circuit design. I see VLSI as an important bridge between the semiconductor devices and circuits I study in Electrical Engineering and the larger digital systems I explore through FPGA and architecture-oriented work.

Ultimately, I hope to develop into an engineer who can understand a semiconductor system across multiple layers—from the physical behavior of devices, to analog and mixed-signal circuit blocks, to RTL and system architecture. Rather than limiting myself to a single layer of hardware design, I want to build the breadth necessary to understand how these layers interact and the depth to contribute meaningfully to the design and development of real semiconductor products.
